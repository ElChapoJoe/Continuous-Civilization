# Changelog

Versions follow `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`. The number in brackets is the mod's internal version in `continuous-civ.modinfo`.

Saves from one beta version may not load in the next. Start a new game after updating.

## 0.2.0-beta (22) - World Age, Test of Time, Triumphs and Dedications

### Added
- Exploration and Modern wonders from the DLC wonder packs, if you own them: Grand Bazaar, Wat Xieng Thong and Ubudiah Mosque (Asia wonders), Nirayama Reverberatory Furnace (Japan and Korea wonders) and Nan Madol (water wonders). In vanilla they only appear from their Age on, which never comes here. Each unlocks with the same tech or civic as in vanilla. Not yet tested in a game by an owner of these packs.
- Four Exploration and Modern wonders from DLC civilization packs, if you own the pack: Rila Monastery (Bulgaria), Serpent Mound (Shawnee and Tecumseh), Battersea Power Station (Great Britain) and Boudhanath (Nepal), each unlocked by its vanilla tech or civic. Tested in game. The civilizations' own unique buildings and improvements in those files stay limited to that civilization.
- Military victory can be won: 100 Military victory points, then a 5-turn countdown, like the Culture and Economic targets. Points come from the settlements you hold (4 each), with more for conquered settlements, original capitals, the Distant Lands and ideology. Vanilla's rule (25% ahead of second place) only runs in a real Modern Age. Tested in game: reaching 100 points started the countdown and won on schedule.
- Numbers that vanilla raises in later Ages now follow each player's personal Age:
  - Specialist cap: +1 in Exploration, +3 in Modern.
  - Leader abilities, mementos, attribute-tree nodes and traditions whose amounts vanilla scales by Age: double in Exploration, triple in Modern (108 bonuses, including DLC leaders you own).
  - Narrative and discovery rewards from Exploration and Modern stories use their own Age's sizes.
  - Still at Antiquity's values (no per-player version in the engine): the Happiness needed for celebrations and for Joyous/Ecstatic settlements, war weariness, and diplomacy action results.
- Exploration and Modern Triumphs (legacy paths): all three Ages' Triumphs are on the Triumphs screen, and each later-Age Triumph counts once you reach that personal Age, with vanilla's reward. Their progress shows the real count (for example 1/20 relics). The Wonders Triumphs count only wonders of their own Age (unlocked by an Exploration or Modern tech or civic); captured wonders count too. Tested in game: Tuhuoqiang (Gunpowder) and Dawn of the Space Race (Flight) completed.
- Dedications: Major Triumphs' bonuses, which vanilla offers at the next Age transition. Entering your personal Exploration gives 3 Dedication points and Modern 3 more, spent in a Choose Dedications window that opens at the start of your turn (or later on a new Dedications tab of the attribute screen); only Dedications you've earned can be picked (Antiquity Triumphs' from Exploration on, Exploration Triumphs' from Modern on). AI civilizations get each one they earn automatically when they complete its Triumph (verified in AI autoplays: an AI with both Wonders Triumphs got +6 Culture and +6 Production on every wonder, +2 and +4 from the two Dedications; AIs without them got nothing). An AI that spends a Wildcard attribute point on a Dedication (which does nothing for an AI) gets the point back, so it ends up on a real attribute node (verified in an AI autoplay: 5 Dedications bought, 5 points returned). Tested in game: after founding 8 settlements in Exploration, the Four Corners of the World Dedication became available and was bought; unearned ones stayed locked. Its effect applied: towns founded after buying it started with 4 Population, those founded before (also after the Triumph) with 2.
- A shared world Age, after Civilization VI's world eras: the world enters Exploration (Modern) once at least half of the living civilizations are in that personal Age. Costs that vanilla raises in later Ages rise for everyone then, so the first civilization to advance isn't charged more than its rivals: city growth costs (about 1.9x in Exploration and 3.3x in Modern), town upgrade costs (about 2x and 3x) and Influence costs of diplomatic actions (2x and 3x; Exploration's own actions such as Demand Tribute start at their Exploration price and cost 1.5x in Modern). Techs and civics from an Age ahead of the world's cost 20% more and those from an Age behind it 20% less, as Civ VI's catch-up. Tested in game: with one civilization in Exploration out of six the world stayed in Antiquity (no cost rise for it) and Exploration and Modern techs cost 20% more; after a 100-turn autoplay with all six in Exploration and two in Modern, the world was in Exploration for everyone (growth and Influence steps on all six, Antiquity techs 20% cheaper) and not yet in Modern. Only in games started on this version.
- AI civilizations get vanilla's later-Age difficulty bonuses by their personal Age: +10% yields and production in Exploration, +20% in Modern, and free Medieval Walls or Defensive Fortifications on higher difficulties (after Castles or Military Science). Before, AIs kept Antiquity's bonuses all game.
- Overbuilding by personal Age: once you're in Exploration, you can place an Exploration or Modern building over an Antiquity building on a full tile, and in Modern also over Exploration buildings, as vanilla allows after an Age change. The placement screen shows those tiles in blue and asks before replacing, with both buildings' icons; if both buildings on the tile are older, you first choose which one goes, and the side panel shows that choice's result before you confirm. The older building is removed when you queue or buy the new one; while a queued replacement is being built, the city has 1 less Population (each building counts as urban Population) and lacks the old building's yields, so buying avoids both. Specialists on the tile stay. Ageless and unique buildings, walls and the Palace are never replaced, and buildings with special placement (water, river, bridge) can't be placed this way. Older buildings keep their adjacency bonuses until you replace them, so advancing never costs you anything. Your cities only: the AI doesn't overbuild. Tested in game: an Inn queued over a Library; a Kiln over the Blacksmith of a Barracks and Blacksmith tile (chosen in the dialog); a purchase the city couldn't afford left the old building in place; a Tavern bought over a Blacksmith (finished at once, Population unchanged); an Observatory queued over a Library on a tile with 2 Specialists (they stayed; Population 35 to 34 until it is built).

### Changed
- Culture victory target raised from 150 to 350 points. The new Triumphs and Dedications bring vanilla's Culture points with them (Wonderland: +3 per wonder; World's Fair: +2 per wonder; Manifesto: +4 per celebration; a Dedication with +2 per fortification), and a test game passed 150 by turn 130 on Online speed. With the Culture victory switched off in the same game, Science was won on turn 184 and the top Culture civilization had 345 points on turn 170, so 350 puts a strong Culture win just ahead of Science.
- Wonders score Culture victory points by their Age, as in vanilla: 4 for an Antiquity wonder, 8 for an Exploration one, 12 for a Modern one (unlocked by an Exploration or Modern tech or civic). Before, every wonder scored 4 here. Tested in game: an Exploration and a Modern wonder gave 8 and 12. Only in games started on this version.
- Celebrations score Culture victory points by your personal Age, as in vanilla: 2 in Antiquity (after the Entertainment civic, as in vanilla), 4 in Exploration and 6 in Modern. Before, every celebration scored 2 here. Celebrations held before you enter an Age keep their value. Tested in an AI autoplay: 4 per celebration after entering Exploration, 6 after Modern (plus Manifesto's +4), earlier ones unchanged. Only in games started on this version.
- Economic victory target lowered from 12,000 to 8,000 points. In the same test game the leader gained about 85 points a turn and had 8,324 on turn 191, so 12,000 would have come about 50 turns after the Science win; vanilla's leader ended a full game with 4,646. 8,000 puts it near Science. Measurements: [docs/victory-pace-0.2.0.md](docs/victory-pace-0.2.0.md).

### Fixed
- Interface parts that vanilla switches by the game Age now follow your personal Age, instead of staying in Antiquity mode all game ([audit](docs/audit-age-gated-mechanics.md) #17): the diplomacy ribbon shows each civilization's religion (and ideology, once chosen) when you hover it; naval units open the discovery lens while you are in Exploration; the Unlocks screen no longer lists next-Age civilizations and rewards (there are no Age transitions). Tested in game. Left as they are on purpose: the trade route chooser (its Antiquity mode matches the trade rules the game runs here) and the continent lens (its later views are about Treasure resources and ruins left by Age transitions, which this mod doesn't have).
- Victories screen: the Culture tab's bars now span the turns played so far; every civilization's points used to sit in one pile at 0%, because the bars were scaled to the Age meter, which never fills here. The Summary tab shows the Science goal (100) instead of -1 and a "locked" tooltip, which vanilla shows in Antiquity. Tested in game.
- Towns could buy buildings before their tech or civic: a town with a purchase ability for a building type (Augustus's Culture buildings, Factory and Fort Town focuses, the Carthage, England, Gaul and Joseon abilities) was allowed by the engine to buy any such building, also from later Ages (tested: an Opera House before Urbanization). The purchase screen now treats those as locked for you. AI civilizations can still do it (the engine has no per-player block). Found through the Continuous Ages mod's notes.
- Exploration and Modern civilizations played from the start now get their own civic tree (traditions, unique buildings and their other unlocks) once they reach that personal Age: Abbasid, Chola, Hawaii, Inca, Majapahit, Ming, Mongolia, Norman, Songhai and Spain in their Exploration; America, Buganda, French Empire, Meiji, Mexico, Mughal, Prussia, Qing, Russia and Siam in their Modern; and DLC civilizations you own (Bulgaria, Shawnee, Great Britain, Nepal tested in the database; Dai Viet, England, Goryeo, Iceland, Joseon, Ottomans, Pirate Republic, Qajar and Sengoku not tested). Before, these trees never loaded, because vanilla only loads them in their Age. They are vanilla's own trees, unchanged. Until then the civilization keeps vanilla's Antiquity Test of Time tree. Tested in a 100-turn AI autoplay: the Abbasids and Normans got their trees in their Exploration and researched civics in them, America and Prussia got theirs in their Modern, and Mexico (still in Exploration) did not yet. Only in games started on this version.
- Test of Time, as vanilla has it for playing one civilization through every Age, now follows the personal Age:
  - Antiquity civilizations (Rome, Egypt, ...) get Exploration's and Modern's Test of Time civic trees in those personal Ages, with their own Renaissance and Modern civics (Roman Renaissance, Roman Modernization), Hierarchy and Syncretism.
  - Modern civilizations get the Exploration Test of Time tree, with their own Exploration civics, in their personal Exploration; Exploration civilizations get the Modern one in their personal Modern.
  - Civilization abilities change with the personal Age as they do at vanilla's Age transitions: an Exploration or Modern civilization uses its Test of Time version until it reaches its own Age, then its real ability (for example the Abbasids get their Gold and Production from specialists, and the Alim from their unique quarter, in their Exploration); Antiquity civilizations get their Exploration and Modern versions. Generated from the game files by `tools/gen_civ_abilities.py` (base game and civ DLCs).
  - Tested in game: Rome got both trees, Roman Renaissance and Roman Modernization were researchable and Syncretism showed its options. Rome's Twelve Tables yield was 1 in its Antiquity and Exploration and 2 in its Modern (vanilla's Modern version replacing the earlier one). In a 100-turn autoplay every civilization had the right trees for its kind and personal Age (Rome and Persia: Test of Time trees; Abbasids, Spain, Majapahit: their own Exploration tree, the Abbasids then the Modern Test of Time tree).

## 0.1.1-beta (21) - fixes from beta feedback

### Fixed
- Land units can cross the Ocean once you are in your personal Exploration: civilians and commanders after Cartography, all land units after Shipbuilding mastery, as in vanilla. Antiquity's rule that makes the Ocean impassable now ends for each player at their personal Exploration; before, it never ended. (Reported by beta testers.)
- The Distant Lands are open: you can meet the civilizations there (their leaders now appear in the diplomacy ribbon), land units can enter that land, and it has valid settlement spots. In an Antiquity game the engine keeps the Distant Lands closed to everyone, and this game always is one; that switch is now off for everyone. Crossing the Ocean still needs your personal Exploration. (Reported by beta testers.)
- The Religion button (and the radial menu's Religion entry) opens the religion picker once you can found a religion, and the belief picker after you have founded one. Before, it always opened the pantheon screen, because the game picks that screen from the game Age, which stays Antiquity. ([audit](docs/audit-age-gated-mechanics.md) #3)
- Trade route range grows with your personal Age, as it does between Ages in vanilla: +5 land and +15 sea range in Exploration, the same again in Modern. ([audit](docs/audit-age-gated-mechanics.md) #5)
- Embarked land units get stronger with your personal Age: +15 combat strength in Exploration and +30 more in Modern, matching vanilla's 5 / 20 / 50. Matters now that land units can cross the Ocean. ([audit](docs/audit-age-gated-mechanics.md) #6)
- Leaders receive their attribute points for each new Age when they enter their personal Exploration (+1 per favoured attribute) and Modern (+2), as at vanilla's Age transitions; Ibn Battuta gets his 4 and 6 Wildcard points. Before, these never arrived. ([audit](docs/audit-age-gated-mechanics.md) #7)
- These four fixes only apply to games started on this version (the game sets up these rules when a game starts).
- City-state bonus choices now only list the bonuses of your personal Age (Antiquity, Exploration or Modern), as in vanilla, instead of all three Ages at once. If every bonus of your Age is already taken, the other Ages' bonuses are offered so the choice is never empty. The AI's own picks are unchanged. ([#2](https://github.com/ElChapoJoe/Continuous-Civilization/issues/2))

## 0.1.0-beta (20) - first public beta

First release for playtesters.

### One continuous game
- The Antiquity Age never ends: no Age transition, no civilization swap, no reset.
- Exploration and Modern techs and civics continue Antiquity's in one technology tree and one civic tree, ending in Future Tech and Future Civic.
- Personal Ages: each player enters Exploration (Cartography, Astronomy or Machinery) and Modern (Academics, Steam Engine or Military Science) through their own research. Age rules follow each player's personal Age.

### Content from every Age
- Units and buildings from all three Ages, with upgrade paths between Ages.
- Governments unlock by civic, with a Change Government button; AI civilizations change government too.
- Religion, city-states, great people, projects, narratives and discoveries from all three Ages.
- Exploration and Modern resources stay inactive and hidden for each player until they research the matching tech.
- Exploration and Modern diplomacy and espionage actions, each unlocked by its tech, civic or city-state bonus.
- Ideology trees open with Political Theory.

### Winning
- Science: 100 points with an undamaged Launch Pad, then a 5-turn countdown.
- Culture: 150 points, then a 5-turn countdown.
- Economic: 12,000 points, then a 5-turn countdown.
- Domination: capture every rival's original capital.

### Interface
- Neater tech and civic tree screens: fewer crossing lines, rows that line up, straight links.

### Known limitations
See the README: Military (points) and Score victories, legacy paths, diplomacy costs that don't scale by Age, Antiquity-style city art, single player only.
